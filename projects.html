Adaptive Block Diffusion
Inference-Time Adaptive Block Allocation for Diffusion LLMs
Efficient AI, NYU Courant · course project (3 authors)
A learned block-size scheduler for diffusion language models (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B). My contribution: the oracle supervision pipeline - K=4 rollouts over candidate block sizes generating ~90K labeled decoding boundaries, with per-token NLL caching that turns relabeling at any compute penalty λ into a zero-cost analytical argmax; ran the Pareto analysis to compare rollout supervision against the lighter heuristic.

Pathology Foundation Model Benchmarking

Visual Perception in VLMs
Visual Perception in Vision-Language Models
Grace Lindsay Lab, NYU CDS · May 2026 – Present
Testing whether vision-language models (Claude, Gemini) show a human-like attention effect — that attending to a stimulus makes it look higher-contrast — by replicating Luo et al. We show two Gabor patches of varying contrast, cue one side through the text prompt, and check whether the cue shifts the model's "Same vs. Different" judgment. So far the models mostly answer "Same" regardless of contrast and show a left/right position bias; cueing changes how they describe the patches but not their final verdict — so we're now probing the basic contrast task across stimulus sizes, orientations, and wavelengths.

Perception-Guided mCBCT
Perception-Guided mCBCT for Robotic Bronchoscopy
AI4CE Lab, NYU (with NYU Langone) · May – Aug 2026
A two-lane perception system for mobile cone-beam CT (mCBCT) positioning in robotic bronchoscopy. One lane fits SMPL body models to pre-op chest CT and live RGB-D human-mesh regressors, registering them via ICP to transport a lung nodule's 3D position into the OR frame — localizing the nodule to under 15 mm against an 8 cm clinical tolerance. The other builds an OR digital twin from fixed RGB-D cameras — metric 3D reconstruction plus continuous C-arm tracking (SAM video segmentation + CAD registration) to localize the mCBCT isocenter and compute a real-time guidance vector to the lesion.

pico-llm
Efficient LLM Architecture & Latency Benchmarking (pico-llm)
NYU Machine Learning course
Built a small GPT-style language model from scratch and compared it to LSTM and k-gram baselines, then studied how design choices affect it — hyperparameters, dataset and embedding size, pre-norm vs post-norm, positional embeddings, and attention variants (KV-cache, linear, sparse top-k). Finally visualized attention heads and used sparse-autoencoder features to surface interpretable circuits inside the model.
